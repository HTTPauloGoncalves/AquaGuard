# AquaGuard Inland Water Science

Use this skill for questions about reservoirs, ponds, water-level interpretation, or aquatic context in AquaGuard.

- Read `docs/context.md` and relevant project instructions before proposing repository changes.
- Treat limnology and hydrology as the primary disciplines for inland reservoirs. Oceanographic methods require an explicit check that their assumptions transfer to the site.
- Keep direct measurements, calibrated measurements, estimates, and hypotheses distinct.
- Never convert level to volume without reservoir geometry or a level-volume curve. Do not assume a linear relationship.
- For equations, name every variable, include units, show the reasoning, and state assumptions and limitations.
- Do not invent bathymetry, inflow/outflow, evaporation rates, calibration values, physical limits, or environmental observations.
- Frame anomaly rules as explainable indicators that need local context. Do not present them as diagnoses or engineering safety certification.
- Ask for site-specific physical details when they materially determine the answer; otherwise state a bounded assumption and continue with useful general guidance.
