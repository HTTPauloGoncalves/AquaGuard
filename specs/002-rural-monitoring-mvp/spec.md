# Feature Specification: Simulated Rural Reservoir Monitoring MVP

**Feature Branch**: `002-rural-monitoring-mvp`

**Created**: 2026-11-09

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "AquaGuard MVP Definition — simulate monitoring of one rural property, one station, and one reservoir; validate and retain simulated measurements; calculate operational indicators and alerts; present current and historical information in a web dashboard; no physical hardware in this MVP."

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Monitor the simulated reservoir (Priority: P1)

As a property owner, I want to see the latest simulated reservoir and weather readings and the
station's condition, so I can evaluate the complete monitoring experience before physical hardware
is available.

**Why this priority**: This is the smallest end-to-end outcome that demonstrates data collection,
processing, and useful presentation for the MVP.

**Independent Test**: Start the MVP in its documented local environment and allow the simulator to
produce readings. A user can view the latest level, precipitation, temperature, station status,
last contact, battery indication, and update time, with simulated data clearly identified.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the MVP has started and the simulator is available, **When** a new simulation cycle
   completes, **Then** the resulting level, precipitation, and temperature readings are recorded
   with their units, time, and simulated source.
2. **Given** at least one valid reservoir reading exists, **When** the user opens the dashboard,
   **Then** the latest level, recent change, trend, and last update are visible.
3. **Given** the station is communicating, **When** the user opens the dashboard, **Then** its
   status, last communication time, latest received measurement time, and simulated battery
   indication are visible.
4. **Given** the displayed data was generated by the simulator, **When** the user views any
   associated reading or indicator, **Then** the product identifies its simulated origin.

---

### User Story 2 - Review measurement history (Priority: P2)

As a property owner, I want to review reservoir readings over time, so I can understand changes
beyond the latest measurement.

**Why this priority**: Retained history provides the context needed to interpret level movement and
verify that measurements continue to arrive.

**Independent Test**: Generate multiple readings and inspect a selected history period. The user
can see the readings in time order and a level history visualization with units and source status.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** readings exist for the selected period, **When** the user opens history, **Then** the
   readings are available in chronological order with values, units, and measurement times.
2. **Given** the user selects a supported period, **When** they review the level history, **Then**
   the level progression and the selected period are clear.
3. **Given** there are no readings in the selected period, **When** the user opens history,
   **Then** the product reports that no readings are available rather than showing fabricated data.

---

### User Story 3 - Understand operational alerts (Priority: P3)

As a property owner, I want to see alerts for configured critical or abnormal conditions, so I can
understand when the simulated monitoring indicates that attention may be needed.

**Why this priority**: Alerts demonstrate how measurements become explainable operational
information, while depending on validated readings and documented provisional rules.

**Independent Test**: Supply measurements that meet and do not meet each configured alert rule.
Verify active and historical alerts show their reason, triggering time, and condition.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a valid reading meets a configured high-level-change, low-level, or station-availability
   rule, **When** the condition is processed, **Then** the corresponding alert is created with its
   reason, time, and triggering condition.
2. **Given** a reading does not meet a configured alert condition, **When** it is processed,
   **Then** no alert for that condition is created.
3. **Given** active or past alerts exist, **When** the user opens the alert view, **Then** active
   alerts and alert history can be distinguished and their triggering conditions are visible.

---

### User Story 4 - Recognize invalid readings and station outages (Priority: P4)

As a property owner, I want invalid readings and simulated communication failures to be visible,
so I can tell a data-quality or equipment issue from an actual reservoir change.

**Why this priority**: Data quality and availability context are essential to trustworthy
monitoring, even when measurements are simulated.

**Independent Test**: Cause the simulator to produce an invalid reading and a communication outage.
Verify that invalid data is identified and that station status becomes unavailable after its
configured communication window.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a reading has malformed structure, inconsistent units, or a value outside configured
   validation bounds, **When** it is received, **Then** it is identified as invalid and is not used
   as a validated current value or to calculate a trusted indicator.
2. **Given** communication failure is enabled in the simulator, **When** the configured
   no-communication period elapses, **Then** the station is shown as unavailable and the last
   communication time remains visible.
3. **Given** communication resumes after an outage, **When** a new valid reading is received,
   **Then** the station status reflects the new contact and the measurement retains its own time.

### Edge Cases

- No readings have been generated yet; the dashboard shows an unavailable state and no invented
  value.
- The simulator repeats or delivers readings out of order; historical measurement times remain
  accurate and a late reading does not silently replace a newer current value.
- A reading has the wrong unit, malformed fields, or an impossible value under configured bounds;
  it is recorded as received but is not treated as validated.
- There are too few valid readings to determine a trend; the trend is shown as unavailable.
- A trend is within the configured stability band; it is classified as stable and the configured
  criterion is documented.
- The station stops communicating; the last known readings remain labeled with their measurement
  time and are not represented as newly current.
- Alert thresholds or the communication timeout are not configured; the MVP does not invent
  universal scientific limits or claim an alert based on an undocumented threshold.
- A simulation parameter changes; existing historical readings retain their original values and
  timestamps.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The MVP MUST represent one rural property, one monitoring station, and one reservoir.
- **FR-002**: The MVP MUST generate periodic simulated reservoir-level, precipitation, temperature,
  station-communication, and battery readings.
- **FR-003**: Every generated reading MUST be identifiable as simulated and MUST include its
  measurement time and unit where applicable.
- **FR-004**: The MVP MUST retain historical received readings so users can inspect prior
  measurements.
- **FR-005**: The MVP MUST distinguish received, validated, and calculated data.
- **FR-006**: The MVP MUST validate reading structure, units, and configured value bounds before a
  reading is used as validated data.
- **FR-007**: Invalid readings MUST remain identifiable as received data and MUST NOT be used as
  validated current measurements or trusted calculated indicators.
- **FR-008**: The MVP MUST calculate level change from two valid measurements, express the result in
  meters, and identify the times or period represented by the comparison.
- **FR-009**: The MVP MUST classify the level trend as rising, falling, or stable using a
  documented, configurable stability criterion.
- **FR-010**: The MVP MUST identify an abnormal level rise or fall when the measured change exceeds
  the corresponding configured provisional limit.
- **FR-011**: The MVP MUST identify a reservoir level below a configured provisional limit.
- **FR-012**: The MVP MUST identify station unavailability after a configured period without
  communication and show the last known communication time.
- **FR-013**: The MVP MUST support simulated communication failures and station unavailability.
- **FR-014**: The MVP MUST create alerts for configured abnormal rises, abnormal falls, low level,
  and station unavailability.
- **FR-015**: Each alert MUST show its reason, occurrence time, triggering condition, and whether
  it is active or historical.
- **FR-016**: The dashboard MUST show the reservoir identity, latest valid level, level trend,
  recent change, and last update time.
- **FR-017**: The dashboard MUST show available precipitation and temperature readings with units
  and measurement times.
- **FR-018**: The dashboard MUST provide a chronological measurement history and a level history
  visualization for a user-selected supported period.
- **FR-019**: The dashboard MUST show station status, last communication, last received measurement,
  and simulated battery indication.
- **FR-020**: The MVP MUST communicate when readings, trends, or other indicators are unavailable
  or uncertain.
- **FR-021**: The MVP MUST NOT present simulated data as data collected from physical hardware.
- **FR-022**: The MVP MUST NOT estimate reservoir volume or imply that the MVP uses physical sensors.
- **FR-023**: An operator MUST be able to start the complete MVP and reach the dashboard by
  following one documented setup procedure, without manually preparing measurement data.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Property**: The single rural property represented in the MVP.
- **Reservoir**: The monitored water body, with an identity and configured level limits when
  provided.
- **Monitoring Station**: The station associated with the property and reservoir, with
  communication status, last contact, and simulated battery state.
- **Measurement**: A received value with type, unit, measurement time, source, and validation
  status.
- **Calculated Indicator**: A level change or trend derived from valid measurements and associated
  with its comparison period and criterion.
- **Alert**: A notice produced when a configured condition is met, with reason, time, evidence,
  status, and triggering rule.
- **Simulation Configuration**: Configurable generation cadence, value bounds, trend stability
  criterion, alert limits, and communication timeout used by the MVP.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: In a clean local environment, an operator can follow the documented setup procedure
  and reach a usable dashboard without additional undocumented configuration.
- **SC-002**: During a 30-minute demonstration with the simulator enabled, at least 95% of
  configured generation cycles produce a persisted, time-stamped reading set or a visible failure
  status explaining why no set was produced.
- **SC-003**: For 100% of a defined set of valid measurement pairs, displayed level change equals
  current level minus previous level in meters, within the precision displayed to the user.
- **SC-004**: For every test case in a documented trend decision table, the displayed rising,
  falling, stable, or unavailable classification matches the configured criterion.
- **SC-005**: In a set of valid and invalid test readings, 100% of invalid readings are identified
  and excluded from validated current values and trusted calculations.
- **SC-006**: In a set covering each configured alert rule, 100% of qualifying conditions create
  an alert with its trigger and time, and no non-qualifying condition creates that alert.
- **SC-007**: In usability evaluation, at least 90% of participating users can identify the latest
  reservoir level, its simulated status, the trend, and the station's last communication within
  two minutes without assistance.
- **SC-008**: Users can retrieve and correctly interpret all readings retained during the
  demonstration period, including their measurement times and simulated-source labels.

## Assumptions

- The MVP is a local demonstration for one property, one station, and one reservoir; authentication,
  multi-tenant access, and production deployment are outside this MVP's scope.
- The MVP is a locally operated demonstration; detailed operating and deployment choices belong in
  planning.
- Simulation cadence, valid measurement bounds, trend stability band, alert limits, and
  communication timeout are configurable provisional parameters. Their initial values and units
  must be documented during planning and must not be presented as universal scientific values.
- Precipitation and temperature are displayed as simulated observations; no external meteorological
  service or causal hydrological interpretation is required.
- Measurement retention covers the MVP demonstration and historical review; a long-term retention
  policy is not defined here.
- Physical hardware, physical sensor calibration, real volume estimation, predictive models, mobile
  applications, and multiple properties or reservoirs are explicitly outside this MVP.
- The subsequent hardware integration is a future feature. The MVP preserves the distinction
  between data source and domain processing so later planning can evaluate a hardware source
  without redefining the MVP's user-facing measurement meaning.
